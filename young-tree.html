Prune, Predict, Revisit

Case 1 / Develop a young tree

Build structure without rushing clearance

This fictional, established young shade tree is growing beside a future walkway. Train it toward one dominant leader, keep useful future scaffold branches, and manage temporary low branches. The walkway is not open yet. You do not need full clearance today.

An original teaching illustration informed by the structural contrast in your Urban Tree Foundation references. Not a diagnosis of either photographed tree. Future views show plausible scenarios, not measured growth or guaranteed outcomes. Case 2, the mature tree, will be developed after instructor review.

Select a letter on the tree or use the branch buttons. All branches are shown without leaves to make structure visible. Dashed lines indicate removed material.

Compare the whole plan

Branch counts are not percentages of live foliage. Judge the size and role of the affected growth, not just the number of cuts.

Inspect and decide

The blue fork marks a possible reduction location. Final cut placement and collar anatomy are reserved for the mature-tree case.

Predict before revealing the response

Make and explain a decision for all five branches first. Your first committed plan is kept for comparison.

Reconsider and connect

After exploring all three years, change a branch decision if needed and commit the revised plan. Retain your original plan if you can defend it.

Teaching references and model limits

This case assumes a single-leader shade-tree objective, an established tree in adequate condition, no urgent clearance problem, and no hidden defects. Species, vigor, site conditions, attachment anatomy, and actual branch dimensions may change real pruning decisions.

  • UF/IFAS: A Plan for Training Shade Trees
