Prune, Predict, Revisit

Case 1 / Develop a young tree

Build structure without rushing clearance

This fictional, established young shade tree is growing beside a future walkway. Train it toward one dominant leader, keep useful future scaffold branches, and manage temporary low branches. The walkway is not open yet. You do not need full clearance today.

Prune the tree, then compare plausible responses after one, two, and three growing seasons with no further pruning. Follow new shoots as they extend and thicken. Growth amounts and shoot numbers illustrate the differences between cuts; they are not species-specific predictions.

Select a letter on the tree or use the branch buttons. Dashed lines indicate removed material. The blue fork marks a possible reduction point. Green segments show growth added since pruning. Removed branches stay absent in this scenario; real trees may sometimes produce sprouts. The three-year response is a teaching scenario, not a guaranteed prediction.

Compare the whole plan

Branch counts are not percentages of live foliage. Judge the size and role of the affected growth, not just the number of cuts.

Inspect and decide

Use reduction when you want to shorten a branch back to a retained lateral. Use heading when you want to shorten a branch without cutting back to a lateral, knowing that vigorous shoots may arise below the cut.

Predict before revealing the response

Make and explain a decision for all five branches first. Your first committed plan is kept for comparison.

Reconsider and connect

After viewing Years 1, 2, and 3, decide whether you would keep your plan or revise it at the next visit.

Teaching references and model limits

This case assumes a single-leader shade-tree objective, an established tree in adequate condition, no urgent clearance problem, and no hidden defects. Species, vigor, site conditions, attachment anatomy, and actual branch dimensions may change real pruning decisions.
